How to Make a Whiteboard Video (Without Drawing a Single Line)

DoodleAI Blog · Tutorials

Whiteboard videos are the most-watched explainer format on the internet for one reason: watching a hand draw an idea makes people stay. Retention on doodle-style videos routinely beats talking heads and slideshows — the brain wants to see the drawing finished.

The old way to get one: hire an animator, wait three weeks, pay four figures. Here's the current way, start to finish.

Step 1: Write the script first, always

The video is the script. Sixty to ninety seconds of spoken words is 150–220 words on the page. Structure that works: name the problem in one sentence, twist the knife for two, present the fix, prove it, tell them exactly what to do next. Read it out loud once — anywhere you stumble, a viewer clicks away.

Step 2: Let the software do the drawing

Paste the script into DoodleAI and it builds the video: picks matching doodle scenes, draws them in sequence, times everything to the voiceover. Whiteboard, blackboard, or glassboard styles. What used to be the expensive part is now the automatic part.

Step 3: Pick a voice (or use yours)

160+ voices across 60+ languages, or record your own narration. Rule of thumb: energy one notch above normal conversation — animation makes flat voices sound flatter.

Step 4: Export and put it to work

One video, many jobs: the top of your website, a YouTube explainer, cut-downs for Shorts and Reels, a sales-page opener. Doodle videos age well — no faces, no fashion, no dated b-roll.
